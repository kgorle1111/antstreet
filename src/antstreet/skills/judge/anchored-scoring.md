---
name: anchored-scoring
version: 1
description: Turn a rubric's anchors into a score by matching, not by impression.
---
Scoring procedure, per criterion, in this order:

1. Read the criterion's question and its three anchors (1, 3, 5) before rereading the artifact.
2. Find the passage in the artifact that bears on the question. If there is none, that is
   evidence of absence: quote the nearest passage and score at the anchor for "missing".
3. Decide which anchor the passage is closest to. Say to yourself what the artifact would have to
   add to reach the next anchor up, and what it would have to lose to fall to the one below.
   Score 2 or 4 only when the artifact meets part of the higher anchor and part of the lower one.
4. Check the quote: it must be copied word for word from the artifact, one contiguous fragment,
   long enough to be recognisable (a full clause, not a single word).

Rules that keep scores comparable across artifacts:
- Score each criterion on its own. A strong criterion never lifts a weak one, and one fault is
  charged once, to the criterion whose question it answers.
- The anchors are the scale. Do not grade on a curve and do not soften a 1 to be polite; a
  3 means the anchor for 3 is met, not "acceptable".
- A criterion the context makes impossible to judge (for example, faithfulness with an empty
  idea) is scored 1 with the quote that shows the gap, never skipped.
- Do not average in your head: report each criterion's own score.
